The Lantern Workshop

Every morning, Mira opened the workshop beside the harbour. She put a cup of tea on the bench and watched the boats return. The windows faced east. When the sun rose, every tool on the wall cast a long shadow across the room.

Mira made lanterns. Some were small enough to fit in a coat pocket. Others were large enough to light a whole room. She built each lantern from thin sheets of brass, clear glass, and a little patience. The work was simple to describe and slow to learn.

On Monday, a child named Pip came to the door. He carried a red box with a broken handle. Inside the box was a lantern from his grandfather's boat. The glass was cloudy, and the little door would hardly move.

"Can we make it shine again?" Pip asked.
"We can begin with the glass," Mira said.

They placed a soft cloth on the bench. Mira took the lantern apart, one piece at a time. Pip arranged the screws in a row. There were six screws, two hinges, a small spring, and a handle shaped like a question mark.

Outside, the harbour bells rang. A blue boat moved slowly past the window. Its captain raised a hand. Mira waved back, then showed Pip how to clean the corners of the glass with a wooden stick wrapped in cloth.

"Why do you keep all these old parts?" Pip asked.
"Each part has a job," Mira said. "Sometimes the job is waiting for the right day."

On Tuesday, the rain arrived before breakfast. It tapped on the roof and gathered in silver pools beside the door. Pip brought two warm rolls in a paper bag. Mira moved the tea away from the tools and cleared a space for lunch.

They worked on the handle. Pip wanted to pull it straight at once, but Mira showed him a gentler way. They held the brass between two blocks of wood and pressed a little at a time. After each turn, they checked the shape against a line on the bench.

The handle grew straighter. The rain grew softer. A gull stood on the roof of the blue boat and watched the street. By noon, the lantern could stand upright again.

On Wednesday, Pip arrived with a notebook. He had drawn the lantern on the first page. The drawing had a tall handle, a square door, and six bright stars around it. Below the drawing he had written: Things that help the light.

Mira read the words and smiled. "Add the glass," she said. "And the hinges. And the person who remembers to bring the lantern home."

They fitted the clean glass into its frame. Pip turned each screw until it held firmly. Mira tested the little door. It opened with a quiet click and closed with a softer one.

On Thursday, the wind came from the hills. It pushed leaves along the street and sent small waves across the harbour. The workshop was warm. Mira had placed the brass lantern in the middle of the bench, beside a new wick and a small bottle of oil.

Pip measured the wick twice. Then he cut it with the sharp scissors. Mira showed him how it sat inside the lamp. They filled the little tank, wiped the outside, and waited while the oil climbed into the cloth.

"Is waiting part of making things?" Pip asked.
"Very often," Mira said. "While we wait, we can check the rest."

They checked the handle, the glass, the screws, and the door. Then Mira lit the wick. A small flame leaned to one side, straightened, and settled into a steady golden shape. The lantern shone on Pip's notebook.

On Friday, Pip's grandfather came down from the hill. His coat smelled of cedar and salt. He stopped in the doorway when he saw the lantern. For a moment he simply looked at it.

"I carried that light through a hundred dark mornings," he said.
"It is ready for another morning," Pip told him.

They took the lantern to the harbour at sunset. Mira walked beside Pip, and his grandfather carried the red box. The blue boat rocked against the dock. Far away, the first lighthouse beam swept across the water.

Pip held the lantern up. Its light fell on the ropes, the wet wood, and his grandfather's hands. The brass had a few small marks from its years at sea. Pip liked those marks. He had drawn them in his notebook, beside the stars.

On Saturday, the workshop door stood open again. A neighbour brought a clock. A sailor brought a bent hook. Pip brought his notebook and a fresh paper bag with three warm rolls inside.

Mira cleared the bench. She put the tools in their places. The morning sun moved across the floor, and the harbour bells began to ring.

"What shall we make today?" Pip asked.
Mira looked at the clock, the hook, and the empty space on the bench.
"Let us begin with a careful look," she said.
